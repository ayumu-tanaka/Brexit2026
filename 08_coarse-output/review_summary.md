  Recommendation: Major Revision
                                
  The exit analysis is carefully executed and robust, but the asymmetry claim — the
  paper's core contribution — is not established at conventional significance levels 
  under the authors' own preferred inference methods, and faces serious
  identification threats.                                                            
                                                            
  Top 3 macro issues:

  1. The asymmetry claim is never formally tested. The paper's central finding is    
  that entry and exit respond differently to Brexit, yet there is no formal
  statistical test of the difference. The two margins are estimated on different     
  samples, at different levels of aggregation, with different functional forms —
  comparing them narratively is insufficient. A joint estimation framework or
  bootstrap test of coefficient differences is needed.
  2. Entry effect significance collapses under robust inference. The SDiD entry
  estimate (−13.5%) is only significant under placebo-based standard errors — a      
  method the authors themselves flag as inappropriate for count data. Bootstrap and
  jackknife methods both fail to reject zero. The abstract and conclusion still      
  present this as a finding; framing should be revised to reflect that the entry
  effect is suggestive but not statistically established.
  3. Pre-treatment trends fail in the entry analysis, and the pre-treatment window is
   razor-thin. The PPML event study shows a significant negative coefficient for UK ×
   Year 2010, meaning UK entry was already declining before the referendum. SDiD
  reweighting cannot fully resolve a structural divergence. Compounding this,        
  biennial data gives only two pre-treatment data points (2010, 2012), providing
  almost no power to test parallel trends.

  Additional major concerns include: SUTVA violation via displacement to EU control  
  countries, the 2020 endpoint confounding Brexit with COVID-19, and the fact that
  the EU–Japan EPA (finalized 2017, in force 2019) provides an independent reason for
   Japanese firms to enter EU countries rather than the UK during the post-treatment
  window.

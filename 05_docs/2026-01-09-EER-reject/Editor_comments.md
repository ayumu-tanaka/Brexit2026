Dear Dr. Michael J. Ryan,  

I have now received reports from two expert referees on your paper "Anchored Abroad: Sunk Costs, Asset Specificity, and Japanese Affiliate Exit from Brexit Britain". Both referees find your paper interesting and tackling an important question, but they also have important reservations about its execution and contribution. Referee 1 is on the fence between a major revision and a rejection. The referee laments the fact that the paper seems to focus on "null results" by emphasizing the lack of exit of Japanese foreign affiliates from the UK in the period considered. The referee has many suggestions that could strengthen the paper, both in terms of relevance and reliability of the empirics, and related to the link between empirics and theoretical framework used. Lastly, the referee points out that the exposition of the paper could be much improved for clarity. Referee 2, a senior expert in the economics of multinational enterprises (MNEs) is unfortunately much more negative. The referee has important concerns about the empirical analysis, stemming from the fact that your paper does not seem to consider MNE's complex strategies like export platforms, which are obviously very important in Europe and could introduce bias in the estimation of the effects. In addition, the referee mentions the existence of a small but active literature on these topics which your paper seems to ignore. Obviously, your paper should engage with other empirical and theoretical contributions on the dynamics of FDI that have postulated mechanisms similar to the ones you study in tour paper. For these and other reasons, Referee 2 is adamant about a rejection. 

I am obviously invested in this literature and I would love to see more contributions published, but unfortunately I must agree with R2 about the fact that the contribution of your paper is below the EER bar, and its execution presents flaws that would require a much more involved revision than what we normally consider acceptable. For these reasons, I have decided to reject your interesting paper.

While I am sorry for the disappointing news, I hope you will use these excellent reports to revise your paper for submission to another outlet. Thank you for giving us the opportunity to consider your work.  

Yours sincerely,  
Ms Stefania Garetto
Editor
European Economic Review
